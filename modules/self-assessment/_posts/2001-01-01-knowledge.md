# Professional Development Advice

After scoring yourself in each section, find the range that matches your subtotal
to explore targeted development suggestions.

---

## Section 1: Attitude to Statistics

### **6–10: Building Foundations**
You’re still getting comfortable with statistical thinking.
Focus areas:
- Identify topics where you feel least confident.
- Bookmark 1–2 beginner-friendly resources (e.g., *Practical Statistics for Data Scientists*).
- Set a small, achievable learning goal for the next month.

### **11–14: Growing Confidence**
You have a solid base and are developing good habits.
Suggestions:
- Practice critiquing statistical claims in papers.
- Join a stats reading group or lunch seminar.
- Start documenting analyses more consistently.

### **15–18: Strong and Proactive**
You’re actively developing your statistical literacy.
Suggestions:
- Mentor a peer or share tips with your group.
- Explore more advanced topics (e.g., Bayesian thinking, mixed models).
- Contribute code or examples to shared lab repositories.

---

## Section 2: Study Design Skills

### Low Range
You may benefit from structured guidance on experimental design.
Try:
- Working through example design problems.
- Using templates for defining hypotheses, variables, and assumptions.
- Meeting with a statistician early in your next project.

### Middle Range
Your design skills are developing well.
Try:
- Practicing power calculations with real examples.
- Drafting design rationales for hypothetical studies.
- Reviewing sample size justifications in published papers.

### High Range
You plan studies thoughtfully and strategically.
Try:
- Leading design discussions within your team.
- Exploring advanced design topics (factorial designs, block structures).
- Helping others refine their research questions.

---

## Section 3: Analysis Skills

### Low Range
Focus on strengthening core skills.
Suggestions:
- Practice checking assumptions on small datasets.
- Work through tutorials that explain *why* methods work.
- Try replicating analyses from published papers.

### Middle Range
A strong base with room to deepen understanding.
Suggestions:
- Build confidence with model diagnostics.
- Learn about handling missing data carefully.
- Explore more challenging datasets.

### High Range
You have advanced analytical skills.
Suggestions:
- Teach or present on a method you know.
- Explore advanced modelling (e.g., mixed models, GLMMs).
- Help shape data workflows in your group.
